# FaithGuard simple demo

One page for a non-technical audience: ask a question about a company's annual report, an AI answers, and FaithGuard says in plain words whether every number checks out, what it fixed, or why it held the answer back. Then plant a mistake and watch it get caught.

It runs on the same server as the full demo (see [README.md](README.md)) and changes nothing in it:

```
.venv\Scripts\python demo\app.py
```

then open **http://127.0.0.1:8765/simple.html**. The full demo stays at http://127.0.0.1:8765/ (the link at the top right of the simple page).

## What is on the page

1. **Pick a company report**: the fictional bank or one of the five real annual reports.
2. **Ask a question**: type one, or click a suggestion or a figure name. These were checked when the demo started (the template answer to each is sent unchanged), and the page says so; lines printed in brackets, breakdowns by owner and lines that share a name are left out. Typed questions and the AI's answers are not pre-checked.
3. **Get an answer**: *Ask the AI* (a small model live on the laptop; in the stress test before the talk an answer and its check took 2-25 s, 6.9 s on average; Enter in the question does the same as the button), *Write a template answer from the table*, or *type your own answer* (right or wrong). A pasted answer from another AI works best when each figure has its own year and unit, each sentence names one line, a change gives both years' figures, a fall says "fell" or has a minus sign (not brackets), and there are no page or note numbers; we have not measured larger models such as ChatGPT. The AI's answer is cut at 120 tokens. A cut answer is checked up to its last complete sentence; one cut off before any sentence ended (the usual case, as the AI is asked for one sentence) is refused with the reason, and its text is shown greyed as not checked. Ask about one figure at a time.

The result shows the answer with every number marked (green ✓ right, red ✗ wrong, amber ? not checkable, not what the question asks, or accepted only because a cell has the same digits), a big verdict (sent, fixed, or held back), and three plain steps: what was checked against which figures in the report, what FaithGuard decided and why, and the answer the user gets. Every fix says that it was written by the rule repairer (v0), which runs on the laptop, while the evaluated system uses the trained repairer v1. On the locked test, with the rule checker's flags, both corrected about half of the 83 wrong answers: the rule repairer 50.6% scored by code, and v1 50.6% with labels for its changed repairs, or 49.4% scored by code like the rules (D-112). The rule repairer added a new error to 6.2% of the answers it sent, v1 to 0.4% (runs/main/report.md). A fix of the up/down word alone says so ("Changed the up/down word"). **Try to trick it** plants one typical mistake (another part of the company, another year, the wrong line, the wrong size, up instead of down, a small maths slip); **Back to the original answer** undoes it. The mistakes are planted only where the checker can see them, so they are caught by design, and the page says so above the buttons, with how many were left out and why (their repair would not give the right figures back, or FaithGuard reads a number in the answer as wrong and every repair changes that one too). On an answer with a right figure and a wrong one, each planted mistake is judged against FaithGuard's own fix of the answer, unless the page warns about that fix. No tricks are offered on an answer FaithGuard could not really check, one that runs into a known limitation, or a fix it says not to trust.

The header asks the server for its state every 10 s. If the server is restarted, the page says "The demo was restarted: reload the page." **Full demo with every detail →** opens the full demo on the question just asked.

## Two-minute script

Tokyo Cement → *By what percentage did the Group's profit for the year grow in 2026?* → **Ask the AI** (or Enter): it says profit "grew by 49.9%"; the page crosses it out, says the report shows a fall of 25.4% (Rs. 3,459 million to Rs. 2,580 million), and sends "declined by 25.4%" (the rule repairer's fix; the note under it says the evaluated system uses v1). Then **Try to trick it → Use another part of the company**: the Company's figures are caught and the Group's put back.

## Honest limits (said on the page when they come up)

- A line printed in brackets (mostly a cost), given as a plain positive figure, is treated as a mistake. The page marks it amber as a *known limitation*, not as a fix, and offers no tricks on it.
- A fall written in accounting brackets ("changed by (22.5%)") is read as a rise, so a right answer is "fixed" to "(-22.5%)" or held back. So is a rate whose down word is on another figure ("decreased by Rs. 879.2 million, or 25.4%"). The page marks the figure amber as a *known limitation*, not as a fix or a wrong number, and offers no tricks on it. Write "fell 22.5%" or "-22.5%".
- FaithGuard checks each number against what the answer says it is, not whether it answers the question asked. A company name with "Bank" or "Company" in it is read as that column, both ways: a right Group figure written after "Sampath Bank" is called wrong (amber, a known limitation), and the Bank's figure given for a Group question is accepted as the Bank's. An answer that gives another year's figure than the year asked, or another line's figure than the line asked, is accepted the same way, and so is a fix that gives another year than the question asks. The page marks these amber: "the number is in the report, but it is the Bank's figure (or the 2025 figure, or the figure for “Gross profit”), and the question asks about the Group (or 2026, or “Revenue”)".
- The checker accepts a figure when a cell has it, so it misses two forms: a year's total given as a change ("increased by Rs. 3,500,217" on Sunshine, the Company's 2026 profit, not the change from 2025) and a figure with no unit for a table in thousands ("The total assets were 1,284,560,210" on the fictional bank, 1,000 times too small). The demo notes both beside the checker's verdict, and the page marks the figure amber and says what it really is, never "Every number checks out"; when the policy holds such an answer back, the page says that holding it back is right, though FaithGuard did not see why.
- When the answer's words name another line ("gross profit for the year ended 31 March 2026" read as profit for the year, "driven by higher net earned premiums", a second line in the same sentence), FaithGuard can "fix" a right figure into that line's. The page says the fix is not trustworthy and marks the original figure amber ("may be right").
- A figure FaithGuard cannot trace to the report (a return on equity, which neither statement prints) is amber and "can't be checked", not wrong, and is held back; one that matches a ratio of two unrelated cells by chance is marked as no real check. No tricks are offered on either.
- A figure that adds up (or averages) several lines is compared with one line only, and a figure with no year of its own is read with its clause's year ("X (2025: Y)", "up from Y a year earlier"). A year in brackets after a figure ("Rs 13.87bn (FY2025)") is skipped the same way, and a short split year in the answer ("for FY25/26") is read as its first year, 2025; in a question, "FY25/26" is read as the report's 2026 column, and the AI is asked it in full. A page or note number ("page 186") is read as a report figure too; the page marks it amber, not wrong. The page marks these amber as not checkable, not as wrong. When FaithGuard changed such a figure, the page says the fix is not trustworthy.
- Open questions ("How did Sunshine Holdings perform in 2026?") name no line and no part of the company, so FaithGuard can check a figure against the wrong line and mix the Group's and the Company's figures; a right answer can be "fixed" into a wrong one. The page marks such an answer amber ("could not tell which line", or "the fix is not trustworthy" when a fix puts the Group's figure beside the Company's). Name the line and the Group or Company.
- *Write a template answer from the table* refuses rather than guess: a short name that is not clearly one line (it reads "revenue" as Tokyo Cement's "Revenue from contracts with customers", but not as Central Finance's "Net other revenue"), a margin, ratio or return, a misspelt line name (when the checker would read the misspelling as another line, it asks "Did you mean …?", as for "net interst income" on Central Finance; otherwise it says to check the spelling), a short name that is not clearly one line ("What dividend per share did the Company pay?" on Tokyo Cement), two line items it can read, a line printed in brackets, and a year the report does not cover (Ask the AI refuses that year too). It writes one line's figure: read its sentence before checking it.
- A date in figures ("31.03.2026") and millions written before the number ("Rs. Mn 2,580") are refused with how to write them ("31 March 2026", "Rs. 2,580 million"), and so is a question in a language other than English: FaithGuard would misread them.
- "By how much" questions (and "Did … rise?", "What was the change in …?"), a growth rate given with only the new year's figure ("grew 21.8% to Rs. 61.01 billion"), some lookups with "up from" beside them, and some other wordings on the large real reports are held back even when every number is right: FaithGuard's safety rule plays safe there, and the page says why. Written with both years' figures ("from X in 2025 to Y in 2026"), as the template answer does, the same growth figures were sent in our tests.
- Words like "up" or "down" are checked only when FaithGuard works out the change from two of the report's figures. Beside a change the report prints itself (Sampath's change column: "fell by 13%" where it prints +13) or before a word such as "higher", the word is not checked; the page warns when it is wrong. Avoid extra numbers that are not report figures ("the 3rd record year").
- The answer is shown and sent as typed, with one exception: a straight apostrophe after a plural inside a line's name ("stockholders' equity") is written as the reports print it (’), so the checker reads the line.
